# Live game rooms

Teachers open **Game rooms** from the teacher dashboard. Students open **Game rooms** in the SHSAT portal and enter the six-character code. Teachers create and start both modes. Rooms, rosters, questions, scores, shared selections, timers, coins and sabotages persist in the database; browser refreshes and server restarts do not reset a game.

## Installation

Apply `supabase/migrations/202610240001_live_game_rooms.sql` before using this feature. It adds `live_game_rooms`, protected by RLS with no browser/anonymous table access. The existing service-role server connection handles authenticated API requests. No additional environment variables or third-party game service are needed. This migration was applied to the configured Nathan Tutors Supabase project on October 24, 2026; new environments still need it.

The server mounts `/api/game-rooms`. The client uses `GameRooms` for the teacher workspace and `StudentGameRoomsPage` at `/study-hall/shsat/games`.

## Solo teacher playtests

Choose either game, configure it normally, and select **Playtest solo** instead of **Create class lobby**. Survival starts with four simulated players; team races create two players per team and balance them automatically. Press **Start game** and answer as **You · Pilot**. No second account or student device is needed.

The **Play as** selector switches between the teacher view and any simulated player, including teammates and audience members. The exam ribbon also has a persistent **Student POV** selector. New playtests start with **Automatic opponents** off so they stay in place while you inspect a scenario. Enable it for one opposing-player turn approximately every 12 seconds, or press **Other players answer** for one simulation step. Bots leave your selected team under your control.

Open **Sandbox** in the exam ribbon for the teacher controls. **Unlimited coins** lets you purchase repeatedly without earning a streak or spending the stored wallet balance. It is also available directly in the shop. Grant 100 coins, empty the wallet, or grant ten of any registered inventory item. Party mode can be enabled at any time. **Teammates follow my vote** supplies the other simulated teammates' votes after yours; Math still scores per question and English only after passage submission. Pause/resume the timer, submit correct/wrong checkpoints, simulate teammate selections, or expire the timer.

After using an item, **Play as [target team]** in the shop switches directly into its receiving student view; **Return to sender** in Sandbox goes back. **Arm Landmine on my question** applies a real trap to the current or next eligible choice question. **Trigger Landmine on me** and **Disarm Landmine on me** immediately preview the real outcome without changing answers or scores; a triggered preview deducts up to 100 actual sandbox coins. **Test Ink Bomb on me** applies seven clickable splats with the normal 30-second expiry. These effect previews replace existing effects and work even if the opponent is finished or out of items. Clear your team's effects or all teams' traps and effects whenever needed. The ribbon and sandbox remain accessible during attacks.

**Jump to question** moves the selected team to any question, including after a natural game finish. It clears answers from that checkpoint onward, recalculates the score, and clears drafts, votes and effects; the wallet and inventory stay intact. English jumps stay within their original passage checkpoint, which must still be completed before scoring. **Restart playtest** returns a fresh roster to the lobby, clearing progress and keeping your unlimited-coins, automatic-opponent and teammate-vote preferences.

The shop uses original illustrated cards with separate purchase buttons. Click or keyboard-activate the artwork to flip between the item and its description/counterplay. The inventory, target picker and purchase receipt are separate steps. Reduced-motion preferences disable the flip transition. Illustrations and generation prompts are documented in [game-shop-art.md](game-shop-art.md).

Both games render the existing `ExamSessionPage`, including its passage layout, navigation, math entry, highlights, pencil, notepad and review controls. The compact game bar opens standings and game controls. Math locks and scores on each question's **Next**. English retains answers across the complete passage and posts points only after its passage-completion screen. Teammates see draft changes within the passage. Ink Bomb disables the exam surface until cleared or expired. The server rejects answers, ready votes and purchases during ink or a legacy blackout; team chat and ink cleanup remain available. New landmines produce a brief explosion and coin deduction, with no five-second answer lock.

Only the original teacher host can access a playtest. Class students cannot join by code or access its test controls. Simulated players are excluded from student room membership, exams, analytics, and achievement progress. Answers and sabotage use the same server grading and transitions as class games; test controls cannot be enabled in an ordinary room. No additional migration is required.

## Last one standing

- The server takes a shuffled snapshot of published SHSAT Lab questions. At least three published questions are needed. Choose 3–50 questions; a smaller bank uses its available questions without repeats. Default round time is 90 seconds, adjustable from 15 to 600.
- At least two students join before the teacher starts. A round is one math/standalone question or a complete English passage. Passages stay together when sampled, so a requested question count can be exceeded to include a whole passage. Passage time is the configured seconds per question multiplied by its question count. A round reveals after everyone submits the checkpoint or its server deadline passes. Any missing or incorrect answer eliminates the player from the standing group; correct answers still earn individual points at reveal.
- Eliminated players remain in the audience, answer every subsequent question, and continue earning points. There is no knockout screen that stops their participation.
- The teacher advances after each reveal. When zero or one players remain standing, the next question is the final. If multiple players survive the entire question selection, its last question is still the final.
- In the final, correct standing players have priority. The highest-scoring correct finalist wins. If every standing finalist misses (or no one remains standing), the highest-scoring correct audience player steals the win. Ties share victory. If everyone misses, there is no champion.
- The teacher may end a room early; an interrupted survival game declares no champion.

The game uses an original classroom survival presentation. It includes no Jackbox artwork, sounds, scripts, or proprietary content.

## Team test race

- The teacher assigns an existing assessment, chooses 2–10 teams, and sets a 1–240 minute time limit (180 by default). All automatically graded question types are supported, including passages, multiple selection, numeric responses, matching, graphs and dropdowns. Tests containing essays are excluded with an explanation.
- After students join, assign individuals to teams or use **Balance teams**. Every student must have a team and at least two teams must be populated before starting. Empty teams are removed when the game starts.
- Teammates share their question position and answer selection. Selections autosave after a short typing pause. Every assigned teammate must vote ready before moving to the next question, including each question within an English passage. The ready panel names who has voted and who is waiting. Students can withdraw their vote; changing a saved answer clears all votes. Only active teammates count. Disconnected students remain active and can reconnect. During a race, the host can use **Someone leaving early? → Mark left class** to excuse a departing student, or **Restore player** when they return. Excused students remain in the roster with a Left class label, keep their scores and team history, cannot change answers, vote or use inventory, and are excluded from ready votes and cursors. A participation change clears that team's ready votes and invalidates stale ballots while keeping saved answers. Teams with no active students cannot be targeted and do not block completion by the remaining teams. Duplicate or stale votes cannot advance twice.
- English passages have a shared review screen. Everyone votes again to submit the complete passage and post its points. Teammates can return to earlier questions for review; moving forward still requires unanimous approval. Answers in a submitted passage or math question cannot be changed.
- Named, colored cursors show teammates' live mouse positions on the exam surface. Positions are anchored to the displayed question/passage and normalized for different window sizes. Cursors are temporary, disappear on departure, and reconnect automatically. Opposing teams cannot see them.
- The host selects **Watch Team** from the overview or standings to open that team's live, read-only exam page, including shared selections, question position, ready votes and cursors. The team selector switches views without impersonating a student. Both the overview and in-exam standings list the students on every team.
- Each correct question earns one point, regardless of question weight. Math points post per question; English passage points and streaks commit together when the complete passage is submitted. An incomplete or stale checkpoint cannot partially change scores. Teams can proceed at different speeds; the live standings show score and the current checkpoint's first question. Students see their team's previous correctness and streak; opponents' draft choices remain private during play.
- The highest score wins when all teams finish, the time expires, or the teacher ends the race. Unanswered questions earn no points. Ties share victory; speed does not break a tie.
- Optional **Party mode** awards **100 shared team coins for every correct answer**. At streaks of 3, 6, 9, 12, etc., add a bonus of 100, 200, 300, 400, etc. A wrong answer resets the streak but keeps banked coins. The first six consecutive correct answers therefore earn 900 coins total. Math settles per question; English settles the complete passage atomically, including base rewards, streak bonuses and trap losses. The shop shows the next bonus and the last checkpoint breakdown. Landmine and Ink Bomb each cost **300 coins**. Buying adds shared inventory; using is a separate step. Invalid purchases/placements do not spend coins or consume items.
- **Landmine:** choose an opposing team, any unsubmitted choice question, and one of its answer-choice IDs. The current passage and the final question are eligible until that checkpoint is submitted. Numerical/free-entry questions cannot hold a choice trap. The picker shows question numbers, subjects and choice IDs, without revealing future prompts or answer keys. The trap stays hidden in student API responses. A correct answer always survives; selecting a mined correct choice explicitly defuses that mine without a coin penalty. A wrong answer triggers only if it includes the planted choice; another wrong choice safely avoids it. On a hit, an explosion appears and the team loses **100 coins**, capped at its balance. English resolves the trap only after passage submission, after that passage’s rewards; coin balances never go below zero. Each trap resolves once. Reduced-motion users receive a static result instead of the explosion animation.\n- **Ink Bomb:** replaces the old direct Blackout purchase. Seven ink splats cover the whole team's screens for up to **30 seconds**. Any teammates can click the splats; their shared cleanup persists on the server. Seven distinct splats clear the effect early. Duplicate clicks count once, stale bomb IDs cannot clear a newer attack, and refreshes do not reset its timer. The attacking team is named.
- A team can have multiple pending landmines, alongside one active ink screen. Each planted mine consumes one inventory item and resolves independently. Completed teams cannot buy or be targeted. Invalid purchases do not spend coins; failed uses do not consume inventory. All penalties, purchases, clicks, attribution and timers are enforced and persisted on the server.

Games do not submit ordinary assessment results, change exam access, or award test achievements. Their scores represent the separate multiplayer activity.

## Adding shop items and ending rooms

`GAME_BOOSTERS` in `server/src/shared/gameRooms.ts` defines shop IDs, names, descriptions, artwork paths, card labels, counterplay, prices and durations. `GAME_COIN_STREAK` defines the earning cadence. `server/src/lib/gameBoosters.ts` handles wallet updates, purchases, inventory use, ink cleanup and trap resolution. Shop cards and playtest buttons use these definitions. Existing unused legacy Blackout rewards convert to 300 coins per stored item. Existing numeric coin balances are preserved. Old saved mines without a choice resolve on any wrong answer using the new coin penalty. Room JSON stores the new fields, so no database migration is needed.

**End room** and **Delete room** archive the room with `closedAt`. It immediately disappears from the host's list, and connected students return to their room list on the next poll. Archived IDs and join codes cannot reopen it. This is a reversible database archive, not a permanent record deletion; there is no restore button. Normally completed games show final results to connected participants and are omitted from recent-room lists.

## Persistence, access and operational limits

- Every request verifies current Supabase identity, role, enrollment and archived status. Students must be active SHSAT members. Only the original teacher host controls a room. Join codes allow students into a waiting lobby; only existing members can reconnect after it starts.
- Rooms support up to 100 students. The recent list shows up to 30 joined/hosted rooms. There is no automated retention/deletion job in this version.
- Active room clients poll every 1.5 seconds; the host's selected team view polls every second. Browser network throttling may delay visual updates, but server deadlines, unanimous votes and scores remain authoritative.
- Cursor updates use authenticated WebSockets at `/api/game-rooms/live`, throttled to ten updates per second per client. The server relays them across instances using private Supabase Realtime channels, as boards already do. No new migration is needed. The browser does not join internal broadcast channels. Room membership and host observation permissions are verified on connection and periodically afterward; cursors are scoped to teammates and the observing host. The Vite proxy forwards this WebSocket path locally.
- Room updates use database revision compare-and-swap, retrying conflicts up to 12 times. The same protections cover joins, shared answers, team progress, shop purchases, inventory use, attendance changes and ink cleanup. Future question content and nested answer keys are removed from client responses; only the current checkpoint content is sent; trap pickers include future question numbers and choice IDs only. The original host of a private playtest also receives a separate current-checkpoint answer key, shown in the exam ribbon and Sandbox; live class rooms and student accounts never receive it. Single-question survival keys/explanations appear after the round closes.
- Deadlines are evaluated on room reads and actions. If everyone disconnects, the first reconnect finalizes the expired round/race before accepting another answer. The host can reconnect through their recent room list; teachers must advance survival reveals.
- A game snapshots source content when created, so later edits to the question bank or assessment do not change an ongoing game. No participant emails are exposed to other players.

## Team chat

Team chat is available in the exam ribbon, lobby and results. Messages are plain text, attributed to the authenticated student (or controlled playtest student), persisted in room JSON, and delivered through the existing 1.5-second room polling. A teacher can read a team’s messages by watching that team. Other teams receive no chat history, including in the nested team payloads. Students cannot choose another team or spoof the sender in a chat request. Chat remains usable while inked or after finishing a test; excused students can read but cannot send.

The last 100 messages per team are retained, with a 500-character limit and an 800ms per-sender throttle. Message IDs deduplicate retries and compare-and-swap updates preserve concurrent sends. Restarting a private playtest clears simulated chat. Switching POV lets one teacher test the sender, teammate and opposing-team views. No database migration is required.

## Multiple traps and compact readiness

Teams can plant multiple landmines on the same opponent, including multiple traps on a question or choice. Each placement spends one inventory item. Every matching wrong-answer trap deducts up to 100 coins; balances never become negative. A mined correct answer displays **Landmine defused** and costs nothing. A correct answer on a different choice survives the trap. All traps on the submitted question resolve together, while future-question traps remain armed. English still settles only at passage submission. A combined receipt reports every trap resolved at that checkpoint. Pending mines can coexist with ink; a second active ink screen is still rejected.

Rooms store traps in a collection, reading and migrating older single-trap JSON automatically. Neither format is exposed to student views. No database migration is required. Sandbox intel lists each armed trap; repeatedly arming a test mine now preserves earlier mines.

Ink splats disappear locally on click and retain their positions while teammates clear the rest. Saves run concurrently; unsuccessful clears reappear for retry. The server still controls when the exam unlocks. Readiness is a compact control in the sticky game header, with a highlighted teammate-ready message, ready/undo button and expandable roster. The control remains visible while scrolling.

## Question feedback and shared exam tools

In a team race, unanimous readiness locks each passage answer and immediately reveals Correct/Incorrect in the sticky game header. The live streak counter increases on a correct answer and resets on an incorrect answer. Teammates can review revealed questions, but their answers are locked on the client and server. Revisiting a question cannot award another streak or reward. Math feedback appears as each question submits; English points, coins and trap penalties still settle once at passage submission. Newly planted mines cannot target an answer that has already been locked. The final team result also retains the last answer indicator and streak.

Pencil marks, text highlights, eliminated choices and notepad text now synchronize within the active team's question/passage. Each player owns a separate annotation layer, so simultaneous edits merge and undo/clear affects only that player's marks. Teammate notes show the author and open the notepad when new notes arrive. Drawings use normalized coordinates anchored to the passage or question surface. Marks survive reconnecting to the same checkpoint and are cleared when the team moves to the next checkpoint. Normal exams retain their local tools.

Tool edits save in the existing room JSON through authenticated, bounded `tools` actions. Updates are serialized per client and sampled every 250ms, then delivered through the existing 1.5-second room polling. Failed saves retry; local pending edits remain visible. Teacher observers can see a selected team's tools; opponents never receive them. The API chooses the team and author from the authenticated player, checks the checkpoint and participation status, and rejects writes during screen sabotage. No database migration is needed.

## Focused sandbox controls

The sandbox uses a narrower panel with Question, Items & effects, and Session sections. The current-question card shows its number, prompt, answer key and saved selection. Select correct/incorrect fills only the visible question without submitting it. Submit this question casts the team's votes for exactly one step; passage review has a separate Submit passage action. The teammate-selection helper also targets the current question. Survival passages follow the question visible in the exam and retain normal exam navigation.

Wallet grants, unlimited spending, inventory and receiving-side effect previews are grouped together. Timer, opponent automation, participation and restart controls live under Session. Teacher/student viewpoint switching and all existing sandbox capabilities remain available.

Sandbox actions validate the player, checkpoint, question, step and draft version sent by the screen. Duplicate submissions and stale viewpoint actions cannot skip ahead or overwrite another question. Legacy test-answer calls also advance at most one question; only opponent automation intentionally completes a passage. Live rooms and student accounts cannot invoke these controls. No database migration is required.
